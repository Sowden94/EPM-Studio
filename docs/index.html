Methods & accessibility

METHODS & ACCESSIBILITY

Scientific basis. Clear provenance.

Two clearly identified calculation methods

Browser estimates: new drawings use local OpenChemLib 3D conformers, MMFF94 geometry refinement when available, and Gasteiger–Marsili atomic charges. Potential is the sum of charge divided by distance, mapped onto a smoothed van der Waals envelope. This is not an electron-density surface. No molecule is sent to a server for this calculation.

Use estimates for qualitative polarity. They omit halogen σ-holes, anisotropic electron density, polarisation, solvent and conformational averaging. The default browser limit is 40 non-hydrogen atoms. Method details, sources & licences

Quantum-derived examples & imported maps

Surface colour shows the molecular electrostatic potential, V(r), from the nuclei and the computed electron distribution. On the default rainbow scale, red is negative, green is zero and blue is positive. Negative potential attracts a positive test charge; positive potential repels it.

The translucent boundary is an electron-density isosurface. Its shape comes from density; its colour comes from electrostatic potential. They are distinct quantities. Opacity affects appearance, not the computed values.

Reproducible examples

Examples use converged RHF/6-31G* calculations in PySCF. Potential is evaluated directly at surface vertices from the nuclear potential minus the electronic Coulomb integral. Density uses a 64³ grid. Geometry: RDKit ETKDG / MMFF94, without quantum geometry optimization.

These are introductory gas-phase, single-conformer calculations. Potential depends on geometry, method and the chosen surface. It does not alone establish reaction selectivity or atomic partial charges.
